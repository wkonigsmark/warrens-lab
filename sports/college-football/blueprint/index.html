One number per team. Built from evidence, validated against history, designed to get smarter every week of the 2026 season.

The Problem

Polls are opinions.
We need a price.

Preseason polls are vibes with numbers attached — no stated method, no error bars, no way to settle a disagreement. The Index replaces them with a transparent formula where every rating can be traced to its inputs, and every input can be challenged.

The Currency

Every rating is a betting line

A team's number means: how many points better (or worse) than the average college team. Subtract any two numbers and you've got the spread.

Georgia +17.7  vs  Alabama +14.3  →  Georgia by 3½
playing at home is worth about a field goal
Ingredient One

Start with the scoreboard

Half the rating is simply what happened last season — every game, every score, adjusted for who you played. Beating a great team counts extra. Beating a cupcake barely counts.

No style points

Past 28 points, a blowout stops counting. Running up the score buys nothing.

Cupcakes discounted

Wins over lower-division schools barely move the needle.

Company you keep

Every team gets nudged toward its conference's level — a star in a weak league cools off a little, and vice versa.

Ingredient Two

Five offseason clues

The other half is what changed since January. Each clue is graded on a curve against all 138 teams, then they're mixed — the bigger the weight, the louder its voice.

ClueVoiceThe question it answers
SP+ rating35%how good is this team right now?
Returning production20%how much of last year's team is back?
Recruiting (247)20%how much raw talent is in the building?
Transfer portal15%did they win or lose the offseason?
NFL draft picks10%does this program turn players into pros?
The Recipe

W²-Index v1

half last season's scoreboard  +  half the offseason clues

That's the whole formula. The ESPN poll doesn't count for anything anymore — it's shown only as a ★ so you can see where we disagree with the pollsters. Every number on the board comes from the recipe, and every dial sits in one place where changing it takes one line.

The Proof

The time-machine test

We wound the clock back to July 2025, built the index using only what anyone could have known that day, and checked its predictions against how the season actually played out — all 136 teams.

RecipeAverage miss per team

the mix beats both of its ingredients — each half knows something the other doesn't
Honest Limits

What it can't do

  • Trophies are won on Saturdays. Duke rated #56 in July — and won the ACC anyway. They were still ranked too low to make the playoff, and that opened the door for JMU and Tulane to both crash the field. No rating predicts a championship game.
  • Breakouts blindside everyone. Texas Tech, Utah, and Indiana blew past their July numbers by 16–19 points. Nobody saw it — not us, not Vegas, not the polls.
  • A preseason number is a starting guess, not a verdict. The plan has always been to let real games take over — that's the roadmap.
Roadmap · This Summer

Fresher ingredients land soon

  • Right now, three of the five clues are last year's numbers — the 2026 versions simply haven't been published yet.
  • 2026 SP+ projections arrive in August; the 2026 recruiting class and returning-production figures follow close behind.
  • The day each one publishes, it swaps in — and if a source is slow, we can paste the numbers in by hand.
  • One command refreshes everything — data, ratings, bracket.
Roadmap · September Onward

Real games take the wheel

  • The preseason guess fades out. Every week of actual 2026 football counts a little more, and the July numbers count a little less — by mid-October the index is almost entirely what's happening on the field.
  • Rebuilt every Sunday. Pull the weekend's scores, re-rate all 138 teams, reseed the bracket.
  • Check ourselves against Vegas. Each week, compare our lines to the real betting lines — the only referee that never lies.
Roadmap · Earn Your Seat

New clues must prove they help

  • The house rule: every new clue takes the time-machine test first. If it doesn't make predictions better, it's decoration, not an ingredient.
  • AP & committee polls — once they start publishing, they audition.
  • Betting markets — what the world thinks, expressed in dollars.
  • Conference-race simulation — the Duke lesson: predict who wins the league, not just who's best in it.
  • Play the season 10,000 times — championship odds for every team, like a real Vegas futures board.

House Rules

  • Source data never gets edited — we can always rebuild from scratch.
  • All the dials live in one place; changing one takes one line.
  • No hand-tuning teams we happen to like. If a rating looks wrong, fix the recipe, not the team.
  • Nothing ships unless the time-machine test says it's better.
