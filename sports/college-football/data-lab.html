Step 0 — What each ingredient actually measures

Before weighting anything, name the job of each signal. SP+ is a prediction of how good the team is right now. Recruiting is the talent ceiling over a 2–4 year horizon. Portal net is this offseason's roster delta. Returning production is continuity — how much of last year comes back. Draft capital is trailing proof the program develops players. Two warnings from this framing: SP+ already bakes recruiting and returning production into its own projection (double-counting risk), and draft capital looks backward — it validates a program, not this year's roster.

Step 1 — Settle the data policy questions (in this table)

Sort each column and decide: How do I treat the 18 schools with no draft picks — missing, or zero? (Recommend zero: absence of draft capital is real information.) What happens when a 2026 source is still a 2025 stand-in — use it at reduced weight, or exclude until it publishes? Any team whose z-scores wildly disagree across columns is worth understanding before blending, not after.

Step 2 — Pick starting weights (deliberately boring ones)

Start simple and defensible, e.g.: SP+ 35% · returning production 20% · recruiting 20% · portal net 15% · draft capital 10%. The exact numbers matter less than writing them in ONE place (a constants block, like the knobs in build_power_index.py) so every experiment is a one-line diff. Resist per-team fudging — if a team looks wrong, the fix is a weight or a data policy, never a hand-edit.

Step 3 — Combine on the z-scale, then translate to points

The z-scores in this table already put all five sources on the same scale, so the composite is just a weighted sum of z's per team. Then translate to the dashboard's point-spread currency by multiplying by ~9 (one standard deviation of FBS strength ≈ 9 points of SRS). That keeps the new index speaking the same language as the current one: rating gap ≈ Vegas line.

Step 4 — Merge with the results-based index

The offseason composite replaces the crude parts of today's rating — the ESPN poll blend and eventually the conference regression. Preseason: final rating = mix of last year's SRS and the offseason composite (start 50/50). In season: decay the offseason prior as real 2026 results accumulate — e.g. its weight falls from 100% of the prior in Week 1 to near zero by Week 8, the same way SP+ handles it.

Step 5 — Validate before trusting

Backtest: rebuild the composite as of last July (2024 recruiting/portal/returning data) and check how well it predicted actual 2025 SRS — that correlation is the score to beat when tuning weights. Sanity checks: does it rank any G6 team top-10? Does it hate a team the market loves? Each disagreement is either an insight or a bug; the backtest usually tells you which. In season, track weekly picks against the spread — the only honest scoreboard.

Result (run 2026-07-07, api/backtest_composite.py): scored 136 teams on July-2025-knowable inputs vs actual 2025 SRS. Results base alone r=0.765 (MAE 5.7) · composite alone r=0.726 (MAE 6.1) · v1 50/50 blend r=0.772, MAE 5.58 — beats both parents. Blend sweep is flat from 20–60% composite (nominal best 30%, r=0.776); 50/50 kept — the gap is inside one-season noise. Biggest misses: Texas Tech, Utah, and Indiana (all underrated — breakout seasons no offseason signal saw), UMass and Sam Houston (overrated). Duke backtested #56 while winning the ACC — strength indexes don't predict conference-title anomalies, which is exactly why the field-selection layer is a separate problem. Full detail in data/backtest-2025.json.

Step 6 — Ship and version

When it beats the current index in backtest, swap it in as the rating behind the schedule edges and bracket sim (they only consume a ratings map, so it's a drop-in). Stamp it v1 in the output JSON, keep the old index as a comparison column here in the Data Lab, and rerun imports weekly once the season starts. Future ingredients — betting futures, AP/committee polls, injury adjustments — are just new columns in this table that earn a weight the same way.
