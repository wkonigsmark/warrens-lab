# Odyssey Glossary

Seed glossary for characters, places, creatures, objects, and concepts. Entries should stay kid-readable first, with optional parent notes for nuance or historical context.

## Characters

### Odysseus

King of Ithaca and the hero trying to get home after the Trojan War. He is brave, clever, proud, loving, tricky, and sometimes makes terrible choices.

- Parent note: Homer presents Odysseus as a complicated hero rather than a perfect one.
- Image status: needed

### Penelope

Odysseus' wife and Telemachus' mother. She waits in Ithaca, protects the household, and uses her intelligence to delay the suitors.

- Parent note: Penelope's patience is active and strategic, not passive.
- Image status: needed

### Telemachus

The son of Odysseus and Penelope. He begins the story uncertain and frustrated, then grows into a braver young man.

- Parent note: His journey is sometimes called the Telemachy.
- Image status: needed

### Athena

Goddess of wisdom, craft, war strategy, and courage. She protects Odysseus and Telemachus, often while disguised as someone else.

- Parent note: Athena's help is powerful, but she also tests people.
- Image status: needed

### Poseidon

God of the sea. He becomes Odysseus' enemy after Odysseus blinds Poseidon's son, Polyphemus.

- Parent note: Poseidon represents both divine anger and the danger of the sea.
- Image status: needed

### Zeus

King of the gods. He does not always act directly, but his decisions shape the fate of mortals.

- Parent note: Zeus often appears as the final authority among the Olympian gods.
- Image status: needed

### Calypso

A nymph who keeps Odysseus on her island, Ogygia. She offers him comfort and immortality, but he still longs for home.

- Parent note: Present this as captivity and temptation in kid-readable terms.
- Image status: needed

### Hermes

Messenger of the gods. He carries Zeus' command to Calypso and later gives Odysseus help before meeting Circe.

- Parent note: Hermes often moves between worlds and boundaries.
- Image status: needed

### Nausicaa

Princess of the Phaeacians. She finds Odysseus after the storm and helps him reach her parents' palace.

- Parent note: Her scene shows hospitality and courage.
- Image status: needed

### Alcinous

King of the Phaeacians. He welcomes Odysseus and helps send him home.

- Parent note: His court is one of the poem's great examples of good hospitality.
- Image status: needed

### Arete

Queen of the Phaeacians. Odysseus is advised to appeal to her for help.

- Parent note: Arete's authority matters in the Phaeacian court.
- Image status: needed

### Nestor

An old king of Pylos who fought at Troy. Telemachus visits him to ask about Odysseus.

- Parent note: Nestor represents memory and old heroic storytelling.
- Image status: needed

### Menelaus

King of Sparta and husband of Helen. He tells Telemachus that Odysseus may still be alive.

- Parent note: His own long return from Troy echoes Odysseus' journey.
- Image status: needed

### Helen

Queen of Sparta, famous for her role in the Trojan War. She remembers Odysseus' cleverness.

- Parent note: Handle Helen with nuance rather than blame.
- Image status: needed

### Circe

A powerful enchantress who turns some of Odysseus' men into pigs. Later, she helps Odysseus understand the dangers ahead.

- Parent note: Circe is dangerous, intelligent, and eventually helpful.
- Image status: needed

### Tiresias

A prophet whom Odysseus meets in the underworld. He warns Odysseus about the cattle of the Sun.

- Parent note: Tiresias connects the journey to prophecy and fate.
- Image status: needed

### Polyphemus

A Cyclops who traps Odysseus and his men in a cave. Odysseus escapes by tricking him.

- Parent note: This monster scene begins Poseidon's wrath.
- Image status: needed

### Eumaeus

The loyal swineherd of Ithaca. He shelters Odysseus when Odysseus returns in disguise.

- Parent note: Eumaeus shows that loyalty and nobility are not limited to kings.
- Image status: needed

### Eurycleia

An old nurse in Odysseus' household. She recognizes Odysseus by an old scar.

- Parent note: Her recognition scene can be emotionally rich but simple.
- Image status: needed

### Argos

Odysseus' old dog, who recognizes his master even in disguise.

- Parent note: This is a tender and sad scene.
- Image status: needed

### Laertes

Odysseus' father, living in grief and old age while Odysseus is away.

- Parent note: He matters most near the end, when family restoration broadens beyond husband and wife.
- Image status: needed

## Groups

### The Suitors

Men who crowd Odysseus' palace and pressure Penelope to choose a new husband. They behave arrogantly and threaten Telemachus.

- Parent note: Their wrongdoing is not only rudeness; they are destabilizing the household and kingdom.
- Image status: needed

### Odysseus' Crew

The sailors who travel with Odysseus after Troy. They are brave and tired, but sometimes foolish or disobedient.

- Parent note: The crew's choices often test Odysseus' leadership.
- Image status: needed

### The Phaeacians

A seafaring people who welcome Odysseus and carry him home to Ithaca.

- Parent note: They occupy a fairy-tale border between realistic society and mythic wonder.
- Image status: needed

## Creatures and Monsters

### Cyclops

A race of one-eyed giants. Polyphemus is the Cyclops Odysseus meets.

- Parent note: The Cyclopes live outside Greek social rules of hospitality.
- Image status: needed

### Sirens

Mysterious singers whose song makes sailors forget danger and sail toward death.

- Parent note: Their danger is temptation through beauty and knowledge.
- Image status: needed

### Scylla

A monster with many heads who lives near a narrow sea passage.

- Parent note: Often paired with Charybdis as an impossible-choice episode.
- Image status: needed

### Charybdis

A terrifying whirlpool that swallows the sea and spits it back out.

- Parent note: Sometimes associated with real whirlpools in the Strait of Messina, but the identification is interpretive.
- Image status: needed

## Places

### Ithaca

Odysseus' island home. Penelope and Telemachus wait there while the suitors cause trouble.

- Map bucket: known place
- Parent note: Modern Ithaca is a real Greek island, though ancient geography debates can be complex.
- Image status: needed

### Pylos

The kingdom of Nestor, where Telemachus first searches for news.

- Map bucket: known place
- Parent note: Usually associated with the area of modern Pylos in Greece.
- Image status: needed

### Sparta

The kingdom of Menelaus and Helen.

- Map bucket: known place
- Parent note: A real region and ancient city-state in the Peloponnese.
- Image status: needed

### Troy

The city where the great war happened before the Odyssey begins.

- Map bucket: known place
- Parent note: Ancient Troy is commonly associated with Hisarlik in modern Turkey.
- Image status: needed

### Ogygia

Calypso's island, where Odysseus is held for years.

- Map bucket: mythic place
- Parent note: Many locations have been proposed, but the story can keep it mythic.
- Image status: needed

### Scheria

The land of the Phaeacians, where Odysseus is welcomed before being carried home.

- Map bucket: mythic place
- Parent note: Sometimes connected with Corfu, but not certain.
- Image status: needed

### Aeaea

Circe's island.

- Map bucket: mythic place
- Parent note: Later traditions proposed Italian locations, but the poem leaves it mythic.
- Image status: needed

### The Underworld

The shadowy realm of the dead, where Odysseus seeks prophecy.

- Map bucket: mythic place
- Parent note: This is a spiritual journey more than a normal travel stop.
- Image status: needed

### Thrinacia

The island of the Sun god's sacred cattle.

- Map bucket: mythic/possible real place
- Parent note: Often associated by later readers with Sicily, but this should remain optional.
- Image status: needed

## Objects and Concepts

### The Great Bow

Odysseus' powerful bow, which only he can string.

- Parent note: The bow becomes a test of identity and rightful authority.
- Image status: needed

### The Marriage Bed

The bed Odysseus built around a living olive tree. Penelope uses it to test whether he is truly her husband.

- Parent note: A symbol of rootedness, marriage, and home.
- Image status: needed

### Hospitality

The ancient rule that strangers and guests should be treated with respect.

- Parent note: Good and bad hospitality structure much of the Odyssey.
- Image status: optional

### Disguise

Many characters hide who they are, especially Athena and Odysseus.

- Parent note: Disguise tests whether people act justly when status is hidden.
- Image status: optional
