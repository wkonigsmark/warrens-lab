# Odyssey Art Bible

Working art direction for the Odyssey storybook tool. This is a living document: every new reference image should either reinforce the house style or teach us something we want to revise.

## Current House Style

- Medium: polished pencil-and-ink storybook illustration with color-pencil texture.
- Linework: clean dark outlines, detailed but friendly, readable at small card sizes.
- Color: bright Mediterranean palette with blue seas/skies, warm stone, white garments, gold divine accents, olive greens, and selective purples/pinks for magic.
- Composition: portrait storybook plates with soft white margins; characters are large, expressive, and easy to identify.
- Mood: adventurous and mythic, but kid-readable. Danger is allowed through scale, expression, weather, darkness, and suspense rather than gore.
- Historical texture: classical Greek-inspired clothing, ships, helmets, shields, columns, sandals, and natural coastlines.

## Safety And Tone Rules

- No gore.
- Monsters can be large, frightening, and emotionally intense.
- Battle scenes can show weapons, smoke, fire, fear, and fallen figures, but should avoid blood and cruel closeups.
- Peril should be clear but not visually cruel.
- The four-year-old version can crop or choose gentler images; the ten-year-old version can keep darker scenes and stronger tension.
- Odysseus should remain visually heroic but not perfect: weathered, alert, physically capable, and thoughtful.

## Continuity Notes

### Odysseus

- Adult man with curly brown hair and a full brown beard.
- Strong build, often in a white tunic with a blue cloak.
- Sometimes wears a simple gold headband.
- Often carries or appears near a sword, spear, rope, ship mast, or olive branch depending on scene.
- Expression should vary: watchful, grieving, calculating, brave, exhausted, or proud.

### Cicones / Early Battle Scenes

- Coastal city, walls, ships, spears, shields, smoke, and beach action.
- Useful as a visual language for the cost of bad judgment after Troy.
- Keep the scene energetic and scary, but avoid blood or lingering on injuries.

### Lotus-Eaters / Dreamy Temptation Scenes

- Lush greenery, pink lotus flowers, calm shorelines, and a ship visible nearby.
- Crew members should look relaxed, sleepy, enchanted, or forgetful rather than harmed.
- Odysseus should stay active and alert, gesturing back toward the ship or trying to gather the crew.
- The danger is softness, forgetting, and delay rather than violence.

### Athena

- Tall goddess in white and gold.
- Golden helmet, spear, shield, and owl companion are useful recurring symbols.
- Calm, direct, luminous presence.
- She may appear as a goddess in art even when the story text says she is disguised, unless we later create separate disguise designs.

### Hermes

- Youthful messenger god with winged helmet and sandals.
- Carries a caduceus.
- Should feel quick, airborne, and helpful but mysterious.

### Circe

- Powerful enchantress with long hair, purple garments, gold jewelry, and pink magical swirls.
- Should feel beautiful, dangerous, intelligent, and eventually helpful.
- Avoid making her a simple villain.

### Polyphemus

- Huge one-eyed giant with rough cave setting and fearful scale.
- Scary but not grotesque.
- Best used as a benchmark for level 4 danger: intense and monster-sized, without gore.

### Sirens

- Beautiful singers on rocky coastlines, with musical magic shown as colored swirls or notes.
- Dangerous through enchantment rather than attack.
- Their beauty should feel eerie, not silly.

## Asset Naming Convention

Use short lowercase filenames with hyphens:

- `character-name-color.png`
- `character-name-bw.png`
- `scene-key-color.png`
- `scene-key-bw.png`

For final scene art, prefer filenames that match scene IDs when possible:

- `cyclops-cave-color.png`
- `cyclops-cave-bw.png`
