# The Odyssey Storybook Outline

Working draft for a kid-readable, faithful retelling of Homer's Odyssey. This version begins with Telemachus and Penelope in Ithaca, then follows the journey in story order while preserving the poem's emotional complexity: longing, danger, cleverness, pride, loyalty, disguise, and homecoming.

## Story Principles

- Audience: ages 4-10, with future controls for gentler or scarier versions.
- Tone: bedtime-readable adventure with mythic wonder, danger, humor, and tenderness.
- Violence: suspense and peril are welcome; gore should be avoided.
- Odysseus: brilliant, brave, loyal, proud, cunning, sometimes selfish, and deeply complicated.
- Gods: real and active, but mysterious. They may appear openly or move through dreams, omens, storms, strangers, and sudden changes of luck.
- Structure: modular scenes that can become web story cards.
- Map: every scene gets a location node, even when the place is mythic, uncertain, or debated.

## Scene Fields

Each scene should eventually support:

- Title
- Short summary
- Kid-facing narrative prose
- Parent notes
- Location node
- Characters
- Glossary links
- Image brief
- Scariness level
- Themes

## Main Outline

### 1. Trouble in Ithaca

Odysseus has been gone for many years since the Trojan War. In Ithaca, Penelope waits and protects the household while arrogant suitors crowd the palace, eat the family's food, and pressure her to choose a new husband. Telemachus is old enough to see the danger but not yet sure how to act.

- Location node: `ithaca-palace`
- Location bucket: known place
- Key characters: Penelope, Telemachus, the suitors, Athena
- Scariness level: 1
- Themes: waiting, growing up, disorder at home
- Parent note: This section sets up the emotional stakes before Odysseus appears.

### 2. Athena Sends Telemachus Out

Athena visits Ithaca in disguise and gives Telemachus courage. She urges him to call an assembly and then sail away to ask old heroes what happened to his father. Telemachus begins to change from a worried son into someone willing to act.

- Location node: `ithaca-palace`
- Location bucket: known place
- Key characters: Athena, Telemachus, Penelope, the suitors
- Scariness level: 1
- Themes: courage, guidance, disguise, first steps
- Parent note: Athena's disguises introduce the idea that gods may be near even when people do not recognize them.

### 3. Telemachus Visits Nestor

Telemachus sails to Pylos and meets Nestor, an old king who fought beside Odysseus at Troy. Nestor tells him what he knows about the Greek heroes' difficult returns, then sends him onward to Sparta.

- Location node: `pylos`
- Location bucket: known place
- Key characters: Telemachus, Athena, Nestor, Pisistratus
- Scariness level: 1
- Themes: memory, hospitality, learning from elders
- Parent note: This scene helps connect the Odyssey to the aftermath of the Trojan War without requiring a full Iliad retelling.

### 4. Telemachus Visits Menelaus and Helen

In Sparta, Telemachus meets Menelaus and Helen. They remember Odysseus as a brilliant warrior and tell Telemachus that Odysseus may still be alive, held far away by the nymph Calypso. Telemachus gains hope, while the suitors back in Ithaca plan an ambush.

- Location node: `sparta`
- Location bucket: known place
- Key characters: Telemachus, Menelaus, Helen, Pisistratus, the suitors
- Scariness level: 2
- Themes: hope, old stories, danger at home
- Parent note: Helen can be presented as thoughtful and mysterious, not simply as "the cause" of the Trojan War.

### 5. Odysseus on Calypso's Island

Odysseus is alive on Ogygia, where Calypso has kept him for years. She offers comfort and immortality, but Odysseus longs for home. The gods decide he must be allowed to leave, and Calypso helps him build a raft.

- Location node: `ogygia`
- Location bucket: mythic place
- Key characters: Odysseus, Calypso, Hermes, Athena, Zeus
- Scariness level: 1
- Themes: homesickness, temptation, freedom
- Parent note: Keep this kid-readable: Calypso wants Odysseus to stay, but he wants his family and home.

### 6. The Storm and the Phaeacian Shore

Poseidon sees Odysseus at sea and sends a storm that smashes his raft. With help from a sea goddess, Odysseus survives and reaches the land of the Phaeacians, exhausted and alone.

- Location node: `scheria-shore`
- Location bucket: mythic place
- Key characters: Odysseus, Poseidon, Ino, Athena
- Scariness level: 3
- Themes: survival, divine anger, endurance
- Parent note: This is a good place for ocean danger without graphic harm.

### 7. Nausicaa and the Phaeacian Court

Princess Nausicaa finds Odysseus near the river and helps him reach the palace. The Phaeacians welcome him with food, games, music, and kindness. When a bard sings about Troy, Odysseus weeps, and the king asks who he really is.

- Location node: `scheria-palace`
- Location bucket: mythic place
- Key characters: Odysseus, Nausicaa, Alcinous, Arete, Demodocus
- Scariness level: 1
- Themes: kindness to strangers, grief, identity
- Parent note: This scene models hospitality and lets Odysseus' hidden pain show.

### 8. The Lotus-Eaters

Odysseus begins telling his story. After leaving Troy, his ships come to the land of the Lotus-Eaters, where some men eat a strange flower and forget they ever wanted to go home. Odysseus drags them back to the ships.

- Location node: `lotus-eaters`
- Location bucket: mythic place
- Key characters: Odysseus, his crew
- Scariness level: 2
- Themes: forgetfulness, temptation, the pull of home
- Parent note: This can be dreamy rather than frightening.

### 9. The Cyclops' Cave

Odysseus and his men enter the cave of Polyphemus, a one-eyed giant. The Cyclops traps them and kills some of the crew. Odysseus tricks him with the name "Nobody" and escapes under the sheep, but then pride makes him shout his real name, bringing Poseidon's curse.

- Location node: `cyclops-island`
- Location bucket: mythic place
- Key characters: Odysseus, Polyphemus, crew, Poseidon
- Scariness level: 4
- Themes: cleverness, pride, consequences, monsters
- Parent note: This is one of the scariest scenes. Avoid gore; focus on fear, darkness, and escape.

### 10. Aeolus and the Bag of Winds

Aeolus, keeper of the winds, gives Odysseus a bag holding storm winds so the ships can sail safely home. Ithaca is almost in sight, but the tired crew opens the bag, thinking it contains treasure. The winds burst out and blow them far away again.

- Location node: `aeolia`
- Location bucket: mythic place
- Key characters: Odysseus, Aeolus, crew
- Scariness level: 2
- Themes: mistrust, exhaustion, bad timing
- Parent note: This scene is heartbreaking because home is almost reached.

### 11. The Laestrygonians

The ships reach the land of giant Laestrygonians. The giants attack, and only Odysseus' ship escapes. The journey becomes lonelier and more desperate.

- Location node: `laestrygonians`
- Location bucket: mythic place
- Key characters: Odysseus, crew, Laestrygonians
- Scariness level: 4
- Themes: sudden danger, loss, survival
- Parent note: Keep this brief for younger children; the important story point is that nearly all the ships are lost.

### 12. Circe's Island

Odysseus lands on Aeaea, the island of Circe, a powerful enchantress. She turns some of his men into pigs, but Hermes gives Odysseus help. Circe and Odysseus reach an uneasy peace, and the crew stays for a year before Odysseus remembers they must go home.

- Location node: `aeaea`
- Location bucket: mythic place
- Key characters: Odysseus, Circe, Hermes, Eurylochus, crew
- Scariness level: 3
- Themes: magic, temptation, leadership, delay
- Parent note: Circe should be powerful and morally complex, not a simple villain.

### 13. The Underworld

Circe tells Odysseus he must visit the land of the dead to hear from the prophet Tiresias. There, Odysseus meets spirits, including his mother, old comrades from Troy, and Tiresias, who warns him not to harm the cattle of the Sun.

- Location node: `underworld`
- Location bucket: mythic place
- Key characters: Odysseus, Tiresias, Anticleia, Achilles, Agamemnon, crew
- Scariness level: 4
- Themes: grief, prophecy, memory, mortality
- Parent note: This can be spooky and sad, with emotional focus on Odysseus learning his mother died while he was away.

### 14. Sirens, Scylla, and Charybdis

Odysseus passes the Sirens, whose song lures sailors toward death. Then he must steer between Scylla, a many-headed monster, and Charybdis, a swallowing whirlpool. He survives, but more men are lost.

- Location node: `scylla-charybdis`
- Location bucket: mythic/possible real place
- Key characters: Odysseus, crew, Sirens, Scylla, Charybdis
- Scariness level: 4
- Themes: impossible choices, self-control, leadership costs
- Parent note: Some traditions connect Scylla and Charybdis with the Strait of Messina. Keep that as optional map commentary, not a certainty.

### 15. The Cattle of the Sun

The crew reaches Thrinacia, the island of Helios' sacred cattle. Odysseus warns them not to touch the animals, but while he sleeps, hungry men slaughter the cattle. Zeus destroys the ship, and Odysseus alone survives.

- Location node: `thrinacia`
- Location bucket: mythic/possible real place
- Key characters: Odysseus, crew, Helios, Zeus
- Scariness level: 4
- Themes: hunger, warning, disobedience, divine justice
- Parent note: This explains why Odysseus arrives at Calypso's island alone.

### 16. Home to Ithaca in Disguise

The Phaeacians carry Odysseus safely to Ithaca while he sleeps. Athena hides him in the shape of an old beggar so he can learn what has happened at home. He visits the loyal swineherd Eumaeus and keeps his identity secret.

- Location node: `ithaca-eumaeus-hut`
- Location bucket: known place
- Key characters: Odysseus, Athena, Eumaeus
- Scariness level: 1
- Themes: disguise, patience, testing loyalty, homecoming
- Parent note: Odysseus is home, but the story slows down because he must act carefully.

### 17. Father and Son

Telemachus returns safely and escapes the suitors' ambush. At Eumaeus' hut, Athena reveals Odysseus to Telemachus. Father and son reunite, then make a plan to face the suitors.

- Location node: `ithaca-eumaeus-hut`
- Location bucket: known place
- Key characters: Odysseus, Telemachus, Athena, Eumaeus
- Scariness level: 2
- Themes: reunion, trust, planning, courage
- Parent note: This is one of the emotional centers of the story.

### 18. The Beggar in the Palace

Odysseus enters his own palace disguised as a beggar. The suitors insult him, but he stays patient. His old dog Argos recognizes him before dying, and Penelope speaks with the stranger, not knowing he is her husband.

- Location node: `ithaca-palace`
- Location bucket: known place
- Key characters: Odysseus, Penelope, Telemachus, Argos, Eurycleia, suitors
- Scariness level: 2
- Themes: patience, recognition, grief, self-control
- Parent note: Argos may be sad for young kids; it is also a gentle, powerful recognition scene.

### 19. The Bow Contest

Penelope announces a contest: whoever can string Odysseus' great bow and shoot an arrow through twelve axe heads may marry her. The suitors fail. The disguised Odysseus strings the bow easily.

- Location node: `ithaca-palace`
- Location bucket: known place
- Key characters: Penelope, Odysseus, Telemachus, suitors, Eumaeus, Philoetius
- Scariness level: 3
- Themes: identity, skill, justice approaching
- Parent note: This scene should feel tense and ceremonial.

### 20. The Reckoning

Odysseus reveals himself and, with Telemachus and loyal servants, defeats the suitors. The household is shaken by violence, but the chaos that ruled Ithaca is ended.

- Location node: `ithaca-palace`
- Location bucket: known place
- Key characters: Odysseus, Telemachus, Athena, suitors, Eumaeus, Philoetius, Eurycleia
- Scariness level: 5
- Themes: justice, revenge, consequences, restoring order
- Parent note: This is the most difficult kid adaptation point. Avoid gore; frame it as a dangerous reckoning after years of threats and wrongdoing.

### 21. Penelope's Test

Penelope does not immediately trust that the stranger is truly Odysseus. She tests him by mentioning their marriage bed, which only Odysseus could know was built around a living olive tree. Odysseus proves who he is, and they are reunited.

- Location node: `ithaca-palace`
- Location bucket: known place
- Key characters: Odysseus, Penelope, Eurycleia
- Scariness level: 1
- Themes: trust, intelligence, marriage, home
- Parent note: Penelope's cleverness should mirror Odysseus' cleverness.

### 22. Peace in Ithaca

The families of the suitors are angry, and Ithaca nearly falls into more violence. Athena intervenes, and peace is restored. Odysseus' long journey ends not just when he reaches home, but when home becomes whole again.

- Location node: `ithaca-countryside`
- Location bucket: known place
- Key characters: Odysseus, Penelope, Telemachus, Laertes, Athena, Zeus
- Scariness level: 2
- Themes: peace, ending cycles of violence, family, restoration
- Parent note: This ending can be simplified for younger kids while preserving the idea that revenge creates aftershocks.

## Possible MVP Chapter Grouping

For a first web version, the 22 scene outline can be grouped into about 18 reading chapters:

1. Trouble in Ithaca
2. Telemachus Sets Out
3. News from Nestor, Menelaus, and Helen
4. Calypso Lets Odysseus Go
5. Storm and Scheria
6. The Lotus-Eaters
7. The Cyclops
8. The Bag of Winds
9. Giants and Loss
10. Circe's Island
11. The Underworld
12. Sirens, Scylla, and Charybdis
13. The Cattle of the Sun
14. Back to Ithaca
15. Father and Son
16. The Beggar in the Palace
17. The Bow and the Reckoning
18. Penelope's Test and Peace
