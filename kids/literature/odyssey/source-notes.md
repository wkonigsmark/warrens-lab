# Odyssey Source Notes

Working notes for making the storybook more source-aware while keeping the prose kid-readable.

## Fleet Counters

- Odysseus leaves Troy with twelve ships.
- At the Cicones/Ismarus stop, six men from each ship are lost: seventy-two total.
- The poem does not give a complete starting headcount, so the first visual estimate uses about fifty men per ship, or about six hundred men total.
- At the Cyclops episode, Odysseus chooses twelve companions for the cave; six are killed before the escape.
- At the Laestrygonians, eleven ships are destroyed and only Odysseus' ship escapes.
- At Circe's island, Eurylochus leads a scouting group of twenty-two men. This helps estimate the last ship's remaining crew at roughly forty-five men.
- Elpenor dies at Circe's house before the underworld journey.
- Scylla takes six men from the final ship.
- After the cattle of Helios are killed, Zeus destroys the final ship and Odysseus alone survives.

## Named Limited-Role Crew

- Eurylochus: important crew leader, cautious at Circe's palace, later central in the cattle-of-Helios disaster.
- Elpenor: youngest/least famous crewman; his death gives the underworld visit an intimate grief beat.
- Polites: trusted crewman in the Circe scouting party; useful for giving the crew a friendly named face.
- Perimedes: named companion in underworld/Sirens-related work; useful as a reliable sailor figure.

## Early Ithaca Limited Roles

- Antinous: strongest early suitor antagonist.
- Eurymachus: smoother, more persuasive suitor antagonist.
- Mentes: Athena's guest-disguise when she first encourages Telemachus.
- Mentor: Ithacan elder whose form Athena uses while guiding Telemachus.
- Pisistratus: Nestor's son and Telemachus' travel companion.
- Halitherses: Ithacan seer who warns the suitors about signs from the gods.

## Sources To Recheck During Prose Drafting

- Homer, *Odyssey*, Book 9: Cicones, Lotus-Eaters, Cyclops, and the twelve-ship context.
- Homer, *Odyssey*, Book 10: Aeolus, Laestrygonians, Circe, Polites, Eurylochus, Elpenor.
- Homer, *Odyssey*, Book 11: Underworld, Elpenor, Tiresias, Perimedes and Eurylochus in ritual context.
- Homer, *Odyssey*, Book 12: Sirens, Scylla, cattle of Helios, final shipwreck, Eurylochus' role.
- Homer, *Odyssey*, Books 1-4: Telemachus, Athena as Mentes/Mentor, Antinous, Eurymachus, Nestor, Pisistratus, Menelaus, Helen.
